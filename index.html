About

I'm a historian and essayist working at the intersection of migration studies and cultural memory. My research follows the movement of people, letters, and ideas through the port cities of the nineteenth-century Atlantic — Liverpool, Lagos, Recife, New Orleans — and what those crossings left behind.

I hold a doctorate in History from the University of Edinburgh and have taught at Brown University and the University of Cape Town. My writing has appeared in The Atlantic, n+1, and the London Review of Books, alongside two books published by an academic and a trade press.

"History is not the past — it's the argument we're still having about it."

Outside of research, I run a small reading group for first-generation graduate students and am slowly, stubbornly, learning Portuguese.
